# Conversation 1

## Person
- Name / Initials: R.Naveen
- Role: Technical Founder
- Company stage: 4-person bootstrapped SaaS startup
- Where I found them: College startup circle

## Direct Quotes
1. “We started with just ChatGPT, then suddenly everyone on the team had different AI subscriptions.”
2. “I honestly don’t even know who is still using Copilot actively.”
3. “Every new AI tool feels necessary for one week.”
4. “We keep buying productivity tools faster than we cancel them.”

## Most Surprising Thing They Said

They admitted that some subscriptions were kept active mostly because team members were emotionally attached to specific tools, not because the tools were objectively necessary.

## What Changed About My Design

Initially, the audit focused only on pricing optimization. After this conversation, I added overlap detection between tools like Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, and Windsurf because users often pay for multiple tools solving nearly identical problems.

I also realized the product should avoid sounding judgmental because founders often know they are overspending already.

# Conversation 2

## Person
- Name / Initials: V.Muthu Kumar
- Role: Freelance Developer building side projects
- Company stage: Solo indie hacker
- Where I found them: Internship contact

## Direct Quotes
1. “I subscribe to AI tools impulsively when I see people hype them on Twitter.”
2. “I’m paying for both Claude and ChatGPT because I trust them for different things.”
3. “I tried tracking subscriptions in Notion once and gave up after two weeks.”
4. “The problem isn’t the cost of one tool, it’s the total stack growing silently.”

## Most Surprising Thing They Said

They said the monthly cost never feels painful individually, but the combined total becomes shocking when viewed together.

## What Changed About My Design

Originally, I planned to show only total monthly spend. After this conversation, I added:
- annual savings calculations,
- category-based grouping,
- and “biggest waste area” highlights

because users react more emotionally to yearly spending than monthly subscriptions.

I also simplified the onboarding flow because solo founders do not want long setup processes.

# Conversation 3

## Person
- Name / Initials: S.Dharshan
- Role: Engineering Intern building an AI SaaS project
- Company stage: Early-stage student startup
- Where I found them: Friend building a SaaS side project

## Direct Quotes
1. “We bought Windsurf because everyone said it was the future, but most of us still use VS Code normally.”
2. “Nobody remembers which AI subscriptions are paid personally versus by the team.”
3. “We’re experimenting so fast that spending decisions are basically chaotic.”
4. “I would actually use a tool that tells me which subscriptions are overlapping.”

## Most Surprising Thing They Said

They described AI subscription spending as “chaotic experimentation” instead of intentional budgeting.

That framing changed how I thought about the product completely.

## What Changed About My Design

Initially, I positioned TrackSpend AI as a cost-cutting tool. After this conversation, I repositioned it more as:
- an AI stack visibility tool,
- with optimization recommendations as a secondary layer.

I also added:
- efficiency scoring,
- overlap recommendations,
- and actionable next-step suggestions

instead of only showing raw savings numbers.
